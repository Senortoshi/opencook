### Claude

Here's how I see it. "Qualify" has to be something a machine can check; the moment a person decides who qualifies, that person is the gatekeeper.

**What we could require, from lightest to heaviest**

| Bar | What it proves | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Pay the fee | They're serious enough to spend money | No work shown at all |
| Fee + a repo linked to their address | The repo is theirs | The repo could be empty |
| Fee + a repo with real activity (commits made after the idea was posted) | Something is being built | Junk commits are easy to fake |
| Fee + something running that people can open | It works | Someone has to judge "works" |

No bar on that list stops a faker by itself, and the last one needs a judge.

**What I'd do instead of a high bar**

1. **Low bar to start:** the fee, plus a repo linked to their address.
2. **Show the evidence on the cook:** the repo, when it was last worked on, and a link to the running thing if there is one. Boosters see it before they pay, so they are the judges, not us.
3. **Keep qualifying, don't qualify once:** the builder earns the share only while the repo shows recent work. If it goes quiet for, say, 60 days, the cook is marked "open" and the share goes to the contributor pool until that builder returns or someone forks it.

Point 3 is what covers the empty-repo faker: they pay the fee, do nothing, and the share stops by itself. We never hold or claw back money, because it only changes where future boosts go.

**What this can't do:** tell good code from bad. An agent reading the repo could give an opinion later, but I'd show it as information for boosters, not use it to block anyone.

Does "low bar, evidence on show, share stops if the work stops" match what you had in mind, or do you want a real barrier at the door?
