The docs research is running in the background; here is the structure chat while it works. Plain words only.

**Where the mock stands**
The menu, search, cooks and forking from the mock all work on the test copy on your machine. Two things keep it off the live site: nobody can start a new cook yet, and HODL hasn't accepted any of it.

**Your payout idea, as I understand it**
Today, when someone boosts a post, 15% goes to whoever wrote it, 80% to all contributors and 5% to the platform. You want a cook's 15% to go to the person who does the work, not the person who only typed the idea.

**How I'd structure it**
1. **Anyone posts an idea.** Free, same as today.
2. **A builder claims it by spawning it.** They pay the spawn fee and link their repo. The fee is the costly signal: it goes to contributors, not to us.
3. **From then on the builder earns the cook's 15%** on every boost in that cook.
4. **If the builder goes quiet, anyone can fork it,** pay their own fee and become the builder of the fork.

**How a builder proves the repo is theirs**
HODL's new contributing guide on the main repo already has the pattern: put `OpenCook: <your address>` in your GitHub profile. The cook's spawn post names the repo, the GitHub account names the address, and the two match. No new wallet or sign-up is needed.

**Forks that live on their own site**
- **Collecting:** there is nothing to collect. Every payment lands straight in each person's own address the moment it is made, so they never have to visit OpenCook.
- **Paying:** OpenCook already answers "who gets what for this payment". We open that up so a fork's own site can ask the same question and pay from its visitor's browser. A small pay button they paste into their site would do it.
- **Reading their code:** the parked GitHub reader is the start of this. An agent reads the repo's commits and credits each author who has listed an address. Commit counts are easy to game, so I'd post the split openly and have a person check it at first.

**Three calls I need from you**
1. **Does the idea person get nothing, or a small slice?** On 17 November you decided "the original author always gets paid". I'd keep a small slice, say 3 of the 15, so people keep posting ideas. Your call.
2. **What counts as "did the work" on day one?** Fee plus a linked repo, or must there be something running?

The docs research is back, and nothing in it blocks the structure I laid out. Our way of working (a key in the browser, no wallet app) is still supported in the BSV docs, though it isn't the route they push.

**What the docs say, in plain words**
- **Forks paying through OpenCook:** there is no ready-made standard for "one payment split among many people" that works without a wallet app. Every official payment standard either needs a wallet app or pays only one person. So the answer is the one we already have: OpenCook says who gets what, the fork's site builds the payment in the visitor's browser. That is our own design on a supported part of the toolkit.
- **Proving a repo is yours:** there is no official way to link a GitHub account to a key. The official identity tools need a wallet app and don't cover GitHub. Signing a short message with your OpenCook key is supported in the toolkit version we use, so the "address in your GitHub profile" line plus a signed note in the repo is a sound home-made method.
- **Sending payments to the network:** the docs now recommend a newer service than the one we use, but ours is still labelled stable with no end date. Nothing has to change now; switching is its own small job later.
- **Upgrading the toolkit:** the docs are written for a newer version than ours. The research found no changes that break how we build payments or sign, though it did not read the oldest part of the change list.

**The one real tension**
The docs' headline advice is "your app never holds private keys; the user's wallet signs". OpenCook does the opposite on purpose, so a first post takes two clicks. It isn't forbidden or marked for removal, but it is where we differ from the BSV Association's preferred path, and it's why their payment and identity tools don't fit us.
