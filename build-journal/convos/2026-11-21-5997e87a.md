**What I found reading its in-flight code.** The plan calls for an independent audit of each step, so these are for that terminal to fix before it commits:

1. **A post can double as a link proof.** A post signature covers the raw post text, and the link proof is the account key's signature over `opencook post link:<post key>` with the same signing scheme. If anyone posts that exact text, their public, on-chain signature lets the holder of that post key attach posts to their account. The fix is to make the link message something a post can never be, for example by including a control character that `hasDisallowedControlChars` already rejects in posts — that helper's exact rule needs checking first. The string becomes permanent once the client ships.
2. **With `ACCOUNT_TAG_SECRET` unset, linked posts are accepted but no tag is stored.** The proof is discarded, so those posts can never be linked back to their account later. It is safer to refuse linked posts while the feature is off.
3. **Existing posts have no `post_links` rows.** Step 2 (counting by tag) needs either a backfill from `posts.pubkey` or an explicit fallback, or old and new posts by the same account will count separately.
4. **The secret can never change.** Changing `ACCOUNT_TAG_SECRET` orphans every stored tag. This should be stated in `.env.example`, where the variable is not yet listed.
5. **The rate limit is keyed on the claimed account before the proof is checked.** Someone can spend another account's 10 posts a minute by naming its public key. The same weakness already exists on the unlinked path, so this is not a regression.

The rest reads sound: the proof is verified then dropped, the tag lives in a separate table the feed never reads, the post and tag are inserted in one transaction, and unlinked posts behave as before.
