## The path

- **No outside wallet.** The built-in account stays, and each post is signed by a fresh key made from the user's one root key.
- **Only the holder can compute the link.** The derivation uses a setting where nobody outside can tie a post key to the root or to another post. The existing collectibles derivation uses the opposite setting and must not be reused.
- **The recovery file stays as it is.** On a new device the app re-derives the keys in order and matches them against the public keys already in the feed.
- **The server holds a private link.** It is used only for the boot price, free boots, rate limits and your own earnings view, and never appears in the feed or on-chain.

## Why not "nobody can link, not even the server"

The server already sees the IP address on every post and would see your set of addresses on every balance check, so it could link them anyway. You would lose boot pricing, free boots and rate limits and get no real protection for it.

## A correction

I told you earlier that per-post keys would break the square-root fairness weight. That was wrong. The code adds up weight per post, so a person's share of the pool is the same either way.

## What is still hard

- **The wallet:** today it reads and spends one address, so money on post addresses would be invisible until the wallet is rebuilt to handle many.
- **Dust:** shares would be paid per post, not per person. Tiny shares round to zero or cost more to spend than they hold, so someone with many posts could earn less than today.
- **Spending:** using several post addresses in one payment links them publicly. This hides who posted, not where the money goes.
- **Not hidden at all:** writing style and timing.
- **Unreviewed:** the private proof to the server is our own design, and the derivation was read in the SDK source, not run.

This reopens four settled decisions: no key rotation, one address for money, per-key revenue rules, and rebuilding from the chain alone. The 1Sat wallet can derive a key per post, but it does not track plain payments to those addresses and is untested in in-app browsers, so it is not a substitute.

### Claude

**Teranode can't run *on* Saito, but it can run *with* it.** This is from what I know of the two designs; I haven't checked current docs for either project.

**Why not "on"**
- Teranode is BSV's node software: a set of services (transaction validation, block assembly, storage) that run on ordinary servers and follow BSV's proof-of-work rules.
- Saito is a separate chain with its own consensus, which pays nodes for routing transactions. It is not a computer you deploy software onto, so there is nothing for Teranode to run on.
- The two chains can't share blocks or consensus. Saito also prunes old data by design, while BSV keeps everything.

**How "with" could work**
1. **Same operator, two jobs.** A Saito node operator also runs Teranode, or just its lighter read and broadcast services. Saito's routing payments fund the machine, and the machine serves BSV data and broadcasts BSV transactions for users. This is Saito's own pitch: pay for the open infrastructure that other chains leave unfunded.
2. **A BSV module in Saito.** Saito apps are modules that run in the browser and on nodes, and Saito has made modules for other chains' coins before. A BSV module would hold a BSV key in the Saito wallet and talk to a Teranode endpoint for balances and broadcasting.
3. **Saito as the message layer.** Saito carries the peer-to-peer traffic that BSV apps need off-chain, such as handing transactions between users, chat and app data. BSV with Teranode remains the permanent record and the money.

**The catch:** a full Teranode is built for very large blocks and needs heavy hardware, so only big operators would run all of it. Most Saito nodes would point at someone else's Teranode, and you would be trusting that operator for your BSV data.

Is this for OpenCook, or a separate idea? For OpenCook, option 3 is the only one I can see fitting.

### Claude

Researching the open question the plan is stuck on: how to price spawning and forking so it filters serious from non-serious. Two research passes are running in parallel.

The fork-pricing research is back; waiting on the second pass about creation fees before I give you the answer.

The research points to a low price set in dollars for both spawning and forking, and it argues against the 1 BSV fork fee.

**What the evidence says**
- **A fee does stop floods.** pump.fun dropped its roughly $3 creation fee in August 2024 and got over 10,000 new tokens in three hours. HODL is right that spawning must cost something.
- **A fee does not buy quality.** With that fee in place, only about 1.4% of those tokens went anywhere.
- **Small fees lose a lot of people.** Farcaster reported about half of sign-ups leaving at a $2 payment screen.
- **Nobody charges more to fork than to start fresh.** Every platform the research found makes forking free or the same price as creating.
- **People skip paying the original when they can.** When NFT royalties became optional, about 18% of traders still paid them.

**What that means for 1 BSV per fork**
- 1 BSV was $14.06 on 21 November, and it ranged from $10.85 to $22.18 over the past year.
- A boot costs $0.00014 to $0.035, so a fork would cost 400 times the dearest boot.
- Accounts are free and anonymous, so the cheap move is to start a fresh cook, paste the idea and not name the parent. Declared forks would be rare and the original author would get nothing.
- The closest BSV precedent is Twetch, where a reply cost $0.02 with $0.01 going to the parent author.

**The path I'd take**
1. **Spawn:** a dollar price converted when paying, starting around $1. Paid boots from anyone count toward it; free boots do not.
2. **Fork:** the same price as a spawn or less, with 15 to 20% going to the original author in the same transaction.
3. **Make a declared fork the better deal:** it is listed under its parent and reaches the parent's readers. A copy that hides its parent gets neither.
4. **A limit on how many cooks one account or connection can start per day**, as a second layer.

The $1 start is my judgement. The research found no best price level, only "not pennies" and "start low and adjust".

**Two leaks in our own design**
- A spawner who is already a contributor gets part of their own fee back, so heavy contributors pay less.
- Because nothing is held, money put toward a cook that never reaches its price cannot be returned.

The findings and sources are in a new section of [the plan](https://claude.ai/artifact/91NcHio8aSB1ezeSiaL88k). The plan still says 1 BSV per fork, because that was your call.

Here is how the Bootboard works in the live code.

**The spotlight**
- There is one spotlight slot. Booting a post puts it there, and the next boot by anyone knocks it off.
- Each post keeps a count of how many times it has been booted, and the board keeps a history with a "boot again" button.

**The price**
- It is the same for everyone at any moment: 156 sats for each active contributor, with a minimum of 1,000 and a maximum of 250,000 sats. An active contributor is an account with 3 or more posts in the last 30 days.
- At $14.06 per BSV that is $0.00014 to $0.035.
- Each account's first 15 boots are free. The server pays for those at the minimum price, with a cap of 40 free boots per connection per day.

**Where a paid boot's money goes** (one transaction, nothing held)

| Share | Goes to |
| --- | --- |
| 15% | The author of the booted post |
| 80% | The pool: everyone with posts since launch, by weight |
| 5% | The platform address |

- A post's weight in the pool grows with its boots, at a slowing rate, and halves every 30 days.
- Rounding leftovers go to the booted post's author.
- The booter signs the boot, the server checks the transaction, and the payouts are recorded from what actually landed on-chain.

**What this means for spawn and fork**

The split already does what you and HODL want, so both could be boots:

- **Fork = a paid boot on the parent's founding idea.** The original author gets the 15% automatically, contributors get 80%, and no new split rule or on-chain record is needed.
- **Spawn = paid boots on the new founding idea** adding up to the spawn price. Anyone's paid boots count; free ones do not.

One catch on spawn: a founder who pays the whole price alone gets the 15% author share back, plus their pool share. So a solo spawn really costs about 85% of the price. Boots from other people do not have that leak.
